# Story continuity: the story bible

Status: **proposal** for the owner (2026-10-17), written for the cinematic overhaul. Everything in
"Canon" is already in the game or the design documents and does not change. Everything under
"The mystery, answered" and "Chapter by chapter" is new and needs the owner's approval before it is
built into dialogue. Characters are described in [CHARACTER_BIBLE.md](CHARACTER_BIBLE.md).

## Canon (keep)

- **Mara Venn**, a tide-engine mechanic from **Saltmere**, refused to certify a **flood diversion**
  that would drown her low-town district at the next spring tide.
- She went back to her workshop to prove the plan wrong, fell asleep over the drawings, and woke
  on the floor of a cell in **Breakwater**: an island prison built round a colossal **tide engine**,
  where every lock, lift and floodgate runs on seawater pressure. The engine "breathes" (the
  4.2 s pulse in Chapter One; the surge in Chapter Three).
- **Warden Sorn** runs the prison through announcements, work orders and neglected safety systems.
  **The Watch** are his steam automata.
- **Ivo**, a maintenance prisoner, leaves repairs, chalk "IV" marks and intercom messages. Someone
  freshly repaired the copper pipe to Mara's cell (the opening question). Ivo's steam kicker
  (double jump, air dash, ground pound) waits for her in the shaft.
- The seaplane **hangar** on the spur; the abandoned **warders' village** with its playground; the
  lighthouse on **Knoll Rock**, burning when it should be dark, its lamp mended with new copper
  and "IV" chalked on the lens; the **Pier Arcade** teased at the end of Chapter Three.

## The questions the player asks

1. Why was Mara brought to Breakwater instead of an ordinary prison?
2. Why does Ivo seem to know who she is?
3. Why does the island's machinery respond to patterns in her drawings?
4. Why has the lighthouse been repaired?
5. What is Warden Sorn protecting?

## The mystery, answered (proposal)

**The governor.** Breakwater's engine is not only a prison's power plant. Its heart is the
**governor**: a great cam drum that times the tidal sluices for the whole bay, Saltmere included.
The flood diversion can only be run from Breakwater, by retuning the governor so that the spring
tide's surge is sent down the Saltmere channel instead of out to sea.

**The pattern.** The governor's cam profile, its "breathing pattern", was designed thirty years ago
by **Edda Venn**, Mara's mother, a tide-engine designer who died when Mara was twelve. Mara grew up
copying her mother's notebooks; the spiral she doodles in every margin, and the timing curve she
used to prove the diversion wrong, are that pattern. She never knew where it came from.

**Why Breakwater (Q1, Q3).** The governor resists being retuned: Edda built it with a safety
interlock that only accepts changes made in its own pattern. Sorn's engineers cannot break it.
When Mara's refusal reached Sorn with her calculations attached, he recognised the curve. He had
her taken from her workshop at night (the figure at the window in the opening) and locked in the
cell that sits on the governor's pressure line, with her confiscated drawings in his office: he
needs the pattern, and he needs her where she cannot warn Saltmere. The machinery "answers" her
because her hands and her drawings speak its language.

**Ivo (Q2).** Ivo Brannock was Edda's apprentice on the governor. He knew Mara as a child
(he gave her the first pressure gauge she ever took apart). When Sorn began the diversion work,
Ivo sabotaged it and was imprisoned in his own engine. For years he has kept the prison's old
safety systems alive in secret, and when he learned Mara had been brought in, he repaired the
pipe to her cell so the hatch would work for her, left the kicker in the shaft, and chalked the
route. He stays hidden because Sorn would use him to make Mara cooperate.

**The lighthouse (Q4).** Knoll Rock's light is the governor's **pilot**: its lens turns in Edda's
pattern, and its beam, seen from Breakwater's crown, keeps the interlock closed. When the light
went dark years ago (its keeper, a warder, vanished; his child drew "Papa keeps the light" on the
playground pipe), the interlock began to fail. Ivo escaped long enough to mend the lamp: as long
as it burns, the diversion cannot be completed. He left a recording there for Mara.

**Sorn (Q5).** Sorn is protecting **Breakwater itself**. The engine is decaying; the island's sea
wall will not survive another great spring tide. Diverting the surge down the Saltmere channel
saves the prison and the people in it, at the cost of the low town. He believes this is the
responsible choice, and he has convinced himself that Saltmere's low town "was always going to
flood". He is not cruel for its own sake; he is certain, and he is frightened of being the warden
who lost Breakwater.

**The Pier Arcade.** Edda built arcade automata on Saltmere's pier before she built the governor;
they run on the same cams. Ivo hid Edda's original **pattern drum** (the master copy of the cam,
which Sorn would need to finish the diversion without Mara) inside one of them. Chapter Four is
Mara finding it first, and with it her mother's recorded voice, and the choice of what to do with it.

## Emotional arcs

- **Mara**: from a stubborn professional who trusts only her calculations and works alone, to
  someone who accepts help (Pip, Ivo) and confronts the mother whose work she carries. Her dry
  humour stays; it gets warmer.
- **Pip**: from a frightened, overconfident little machine that runs from everything, to a loyal
  partner that chooses to stay in danger for Mara.
- **Ivo**: from a voice and a handwriting to a person who has paid for his loyalty with seven
  years in the dark, and who must decide whether to let Mara take the risk he has been carrying.
- **Sorn**: from a voice on the horn to a man with a believable, terrible argument, who offers Mara
  a real bargain (her district evacuated in exchange for the pattern).

## Continuity rules

- Time of day advances with the chapters: night (One), dawn (Two), late morning (Three), night again
  (Four).
- Pip, once met, travels with Mara in every later chapter.
- Ivo is heard, not seen, until Chapter Four or later.
- Sorn is heard in every chapter; he is first seen (at a distance, on Breakwater's crown) at the end
  of Chapter Three.
- Nothing the player solved is undone by a story beat. Checkpoints never replay a cinematic the
  player already saw unless they choose to restart the chapter.
