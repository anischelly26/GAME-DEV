## Findings, ranked

### R1 — P1 bug: mouse look rotates roughly 57 degrees per pixel; sensitivity setting is ineffective

**Verified by execution and code; highest priority.** Normal-mode Begin and pointer lock reproduce the failure, as does right-drag look in test mode.

Reproduce: start the game; move the mouse a tiny amount horizontally or vertically. Repeat with sensitivity at 0.2 and 3. The harness sends a one-pixel movement to make the result measurable.

Expected: a small proportional orbit, with sensitivity settings changing the response. Actual: horizontal yaw changes by -1 radian (57.30°) for one pixel. A one-pixel vertical movement changed pitch from 0.32 to its 1.2 upper clamp. Both sensitivity endpoints produced the same -1 radian yaw. Normal real-time mode changed yaw from -160.7168 to -161.7168 on the final one-pixel movement.

Impact: precision looking and camera-relative platforming become impractical. Passing the autopilot route conceals this because it uses world targets and reads current camera yaw rather than using human mouse aiming.

Confirmed cause: `Input.consumeLook()` returns raw pixel units; `Game.renderFrame()` at `src/core/Game.ts:466` passes literal `1` as sensitivity; `ThirdPersonCamera.update()` at `src/camera/ThirdPersonCamera.ts:106` treats that as radians per pixel. `applySettings()` updates `input.mouseSensitivity`, but this value is not used in this camera call. Gamepad look also converts into these pixel units; its actual user experience remains untested.

Fix: define one explicit conversion from mouse pixels to radians, apply it exactly once, and preserve independently meaningful gamepad angular speed. Add actual browser-event tests for drag look, pointer-locked look, vertical limits, invert-Y, and sensitivity endpoints.

Acceptance: with the present intended base 0.0022, a 100-pixel horizontal movement yields approximately 0.22 radians at setting 1, 0.044 at 0.2, and 0.66 at 3, or document a deliberately retuned equivalent. A one-pixel vertical move must not jump to a pitch limit. Repeat the traversal with normal mouse controls after the fix.

### R2 — P2 bug: input pressed in the pause menu fires after Resume

**Verified by execution and code.** Reproduce: stand still, pause, press/release Space while the menu is open, click Resume without pressing jump again. In the deterministic reproduction, Mara rises from y=4.620 to y=5.061 within ten steps and becomes airborne.

Expected: menu input is isolated from gameplay. Actual: the paused Space press is queued as a jump. Near a gap this can cause an unwanted movement; E/Enter should also be regression-tested for delayed interaction.

Confirmed cause: `Game.pause()` clears current input once but does not disable gameplay action collection; `Input.queue()` accepts presses while `enabled` stays true. `resume()` does not discard pending menu-origin actions.

Fix: gate gameplay input by state and clear appropriate pending input at transitions. Preserve whether a cinematic has independently locked control; a naive unconditional enable on Resume could break that lock.

Acceptance: Space/E/Enter and controller menu-navigation buttons pressed/released while paused produce no gameplay action after resume. Pause mid-jump and resume continues the existing jump without a new one. Pausing a cinematic must not cancel its control lock.

### R3 — P2 design/logic defect: full solution hints repeat automatically every minute

**Verified by execution and code.** Reproduce: start a fresh cell and leave the hatch unsolved, without requesting hints. The explicit solution is delivered at approximately 150, 210, and 270 seconds of simulation from restart.

Expected: player-controlled graduated assistance, consistent with the design pack's optional hints. Actual: the game volunteers the full solution, then repeats it approximately every 60 seconds. This removes discovery and creates repeated dialogue for a player inspecting the room.

Confirmed cause: `hintTier` is capped at 2 (`QuietCellLevel.ts:641`), but the automatic scheduler continues while it is below the three-element array length (`:703`). It therefore never reaches an exhausted state.

Fix: distinguish user-requested hint progression from any optional ambient reminder; stop an exhausted automatic schedule. Keep explicit solutions on request unless the owner deliberately enables an assisted mode.

Acceptance: an unassisted five-minute idle/exploration run does not reveal or repeatedly announce the solution. Manual hint requests still deliver coherent tiers. Restart resets the hint state.

### R4 — P2 presentation gap, high visual impact: Mara reads as a segmented proxy

**Observed visually; supported by code.** Evidence: `01-cell-start.png`, `03-cell-from-door.png`, and `probe-character-front.png`.

The character uses visibly rounded/capsule-like limb segments, simple ball joints and hands, a smooth hair mass, minimal facial forms, and a prominent blue rim. Clothing has some texture but little modeled construction, fold hierarchy, or material separation. The model communicates a placeholder figure more strongly than a crafted protagonist. Claude honestly labels it a proxy; this is a gap to the owner's quality target, not a misrepresented completed asset.

The supplied reference establishes expressive shape design, recognizable hair/clothing construction, and tactile surface hierarchy. Our protagonist need not copy that character or become a toy. The comparison is about craftsmanship, not literal likeness or equal resolution.

Fix: create one original production-intent Mara model with a coherent silhouette, readable face, shaped hands and boots, clothing seams/folds, differentiated material response, and a proper animation/rig workflow where practical. Retain a suitable gameplay collider. Reduce the always-visible emissive outline after improving actual separation from the background.

Acceptance: compare front/side/back and standard-distance gameplay views under the same game lighting; clothing, hair, skin, and metal must be distinct without relying on a glowing contour. Validate idle/run/jump/landing/interaction transitions in motion; this review did not establish their subjective animation quality.

### R5 — P2 presentation gap: repeated masonry and simple obstacle forms dominate the environment

**Observed visually.** Evidence: `01-cell-start.png`, `03-cell-from-door.png`, `11-ledge-gantry.png`, `13-engine-gantry.png`.

Large areas share similar tiled brick treatment. Small projecting structures and nearby walls show conspicuously different apparent brick sizes. The climb obstruction reads as stacked rounded masonry blocks next to a plain platform. Broad dark deck surfaces carry little authored material detail. Together these retain the look of code-authored construction proxies despite the coherent overall layout.

The player's route is potentially useful; the visible environmental craftsmanship is the shortfall. Exact material/UV causes require tracing the individual meshes rather than assuming the common worldBox helper is at fault.

Fix: polish the cell and one engine-route segment as a material/geometry benchmark: believable masonry scale, deliberate stone/metal/wood separation, structural supports, edge wear, repairs, and a few human-scale storytelling props. Turn the ledge obstacle into a plausible maintenance structure while preserving tested collision and landing clearance.

Acceptance: same-view before/after screenshots show coherent material scale and a purposeful obstacle silhouette. Rerun collision and traversal checks after dressing. Do not fill landing space with detail or rebuild the entire campaign before validating this benchmark.

### R6 — P2 visual readability: steam and water glare overwhelm important shapes

**Observed visually; exact rendering cause not isolated.** Evidence: `15-steam-curtain.png` and `17-exit-gantry.png`, High quality at 1280 × 720.

The steam occupies a large bright cloud across the left/center view; its boundary and the space beyond are difficult to read. At the exit, the water highlight and bloom wash across Mara and the deck, weakening the final silhouette and material detail. This is excessive contrast, not simply an insufficient graphics setting.

Fix: tune steam opacity, distribution, lighting and bloom together; retain a readable hazard boundary. Control water highlight energy and the final camera/light relationship. Do not globally brighten shadows or darken everything to conceal the issue.

Acceptance: at the same viewpoints, Mara, walking surfaces, hazard boundary, and landing edges remain identifiable on High and Low. Supply short moving-camera captures to check that readability survives motion. Do not treat these screenshots alone as proof of a progression block.

### R7 — P2 pacing weakness: the lookout removes control for 12 seconds with no skip

**Timing verified by deterministic execution; lack of skip reviewed in code.** Reproduce: light the lookout service lamp. The probe counts 720 fixed steps with the player frozen: 12.0 seconds. The authored full-route test waits approximately 12.3 seconds for the reveal condition. STATUS describes this as about seven seconds, which is stale.

Expected: a memorable reveal that preserves pace or lets the player skip. Actual: lighting and dialogue serialize into a prolonged control lock before the rope gate opens. The final sequence adds a separate approximately 8.8-second staged ending. These beats may work for some players, but need human pacing validation.

Fix: make the reveal skippable or return control after the short framing beat while the lights and dialogue continue. Ensure skip/early movement settles the gate, objective, checkpoint, and cinematic state consistently. Update timing documentation.

Acceptance: skip reaches the same valid progression state as watching; no duplicated dialogue or stuck camera; first-time players still notice the next route. Measure actual frozen time after the revision.

### R8 — P2 design weakness: engine interaction offers a sequence of actions more than a developed puzzle

**Inferred from authored route/code, supported by screenshots and successful scripted execution; not a human fun verdict.** Reproduce the increment: follow the ring route, use BRAKE, use RIDE, then close the nearby bypass beside the steam. The new movement actions add variety, but machine interactions mostly select the immediately available action. The steam screenshot already displays the solution verb while the objective asks the player to find a way past.

This works as a tutorial. It does not yet deliver the proposed follow-up challenge where the player applies a learned pressure rule to make a choice. Waiting for the shuttle and rides supplies much of the scripted route's time; the autopilot's speed does not establish the claimed 8–12-minute first-play duration.

Fix after controls: develop one existing pressure encounter. For example, expose a visible split feed where one stable setting clears steam and the other charges the crossing piston; a readable mechanical latch retains a safe crossing state. Teach the two effects separately, allow reversible experimentation, and avoid simultaneous remote actions. This is a proposal, not a mandated solution or an instruction to add another gadget.

Acceptance: a first-time tester can explain the cause and effect after solving it, can recover from a wrong choice, and makes at least one meaningful decision beyond activating the nearest prompt. Validate puzzle enjoyment with a person without coaching. Do not pad duration with longer waits.
