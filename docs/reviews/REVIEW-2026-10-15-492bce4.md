### R1 — P1: sustained rendering cost makes High unresponsive; Low is no longer a smooth fallback

**Reproduce:** launch this production revision on the machine above with `?dev=1&quality=high`, press Begin, move through the cell and machinery route. Repeat with `quality=low`. Use the attached benchmark for the controlled route.

**Expected:** the available preset should support dependable jump timing and camera control on the target hardware. **Actual:** High averages 8.77 FPS, about 51% below the prior High. Low averages 28.92 FPS, about 39% below the prior Low preset. Roughly 51% of Low route frames exceed 33.34 ms. Completion alone does not establish good control feel at these rates.

**Relevant changes:** `src/render/RenderPipeline.ts:98` raises Low's rendering scale to native; High now includes GTAO alongside bloom. Current Mara has one model detail level (`src/player/PlayerAvatar.ts:93` is a no-op), contrary to older STATUS claims. These are profiling leads, not experimentally isolated causes. No GPU trace establishes which feature dominates.

**Requested fix:** profile scene/shadow/post-processing costs separately. Supply an explicit integrated-GPU preset or resolution-scale control, with a readable avatar and landing surfaces. Preserve the improved assets where possible. Do not conceal cost by measuring deterministic tests or an idle menu.

**Acceptance:** repeat normal-loop measurements on this machine and disclose resolution. Proposed minimum Low gate: route mean at least 30 FPS with p95 at most 33.3 ms after warm-up, separately accounting for first-use stalls; aim for 60 FPS. Label High appropriately if it remains intended for stronger hardware. These are proposed review gates, not an existing promised specification.

### R2 — P1: first traversal stalls for five to six seconds in both presets

**Reproduce:** fresh context, Begin, escape the cell, traverse the first raised landing toward the lookout. The largest recorded frame in both latest runs occurred at checkpoint `landing`, feet approximately `(0.929, 5.779, 24.235)` while moving/jumping.

**Expected:** traversal continues without a multi-second frozen image. **Actual:** 6.217 seconds High and 5.029 seconds Low, with focus retained. The equivalent baseline location also stalled, but for 1.520 / 1.144 seconds. This is a worsened existing issue, distinct from steady low FPS. The measured stall remains in the averages above.

**Cause:** not established. Investigate first-visible shader/material compilation, texture upload and scene work with a trace. `Game.init` already calls renderer.compile; that alone has not eliminated the observed stall.

**Acceptance:** repeat fresh-context entry into this area at least three times per preset; provide maximum frame times and a trace identifying the removed blocking work. No repeatable traversal freeze above 250 ms after the loading screen is dismissed. Do not hide it by running an unreported practice route first.

## Movement, camera, jumps and climbs compared with the previous build

**Verified improvements:** browser-event tests confirm sensible mouse scale (0.0022 radians/pixel at sensitivity 1), working sensitivity and invert-Y, pointer-lock behavior in normal play, and no paused Space/E/Enter leaking into gameplay. The old one-pixel camera spin was a genuine blocker; it is fixed. Normal real-time keyboard movement is tested separately from deterministic stepping.

**Controller tuning, reviewed in code:** top speed 6 → 5.5 m/s; ground acceleration 58 → 32; braking 46 → 36; reversal acceleration 95 → 62; air acceleration 24 → 18; facing response 16 → 11. This should feel more gradual and less snappy. Nominal acceleration to full speed increases from about 0.103 to 0.172 seconds before discrete-step/collision effects. It is not automatically a responsiveness improvement. High's frame time overwhelms these relatively small tuning differences.

**Jump tuning:** nominal height 1.45 m and gravity 26 are unchanged. Coyote time increases 120 → 150 ms and jump buffering 140 → 160 ms. These increase input forgiveness; reduced speed/air acceleration change horizontal correction. Both normal-loop scripted routes complete without falls. The targeted parkour tests additionally exercise angled vaults, catches/climbs and gap jumps; see execution results below. Autopilot and those tests steer relative to the changing camera, so their success does not prove fixed keyboard input always feels predictable.

**Climb presentation, code review:** real retargeted animation clips replace the older segmented/procedural presentation. Climb duration is tied to the controller's pull-up duration. Hanging freezes the climb clip rather than using an authored contact pose. Tests establish reaching the platform, not convincing hand contact, foot planting, or transition quality. Those animation qualities still require a close-view human motion review.

### R3 — P2 design risk: automatic camera yaw also turns during airborne travel

`src/camera/ThirdPersonCamera.ts:137` enables lazy follow after one second without look input and does not gate it on grounded/traversal state. Default following distance also moves 5.6 → 4.9 m and velocity-based look-ahead is added.

**Executed isolated reproduction:** run `node docs/reviews/camera-probe-492bce4.mjs`. An unobstructed mocked camera cast and a 0.5-second lateral airborne path at 4 m/s produce **24.319° automatic yaw change** in the latest camera and **0°** in the baseline, without look input. This is a camera-policy test with synthetic feet positions, not a reproduction of a failed player jump.

**Concern:** movement is camera-relative, so automatic yaw can change the world direction of a held key during a precision jump. **Requested follow-up:** test normal-loop diagonal approaches while holding fixed keys, without the autopilot compensating for yaw. Prefer suspending automatic yaw during airborne/hang/climb/vault states, or provide a tested alternative preserving predictable steering. Keep intentional mouse look and explicit recenter available.

**Acceptance:** demonstrate successful repeatable off-axis jumps with fixed input and explain the chosen automatic-follow behavior. Do not claim an actual missed jump from this isolated fixture alone.
